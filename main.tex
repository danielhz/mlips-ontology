\begin{abstract}
  Machine-learned interatomic potentials (MLIPs) enable, for the first time, large-scale simulations of microstructural entities with near-quantum-mechanical accuracy. However, the construction of an MLIP for a specific material is limited to highly experienced domain experts from different disciplines: simulation, machine learning, engineering, physics, and material science. To tackle this limitation, we envision the creation of a knowledge graph that can store this expert knowledge and allow for automatized assistance to MLIP creators. In this paper we define an ontology about MLIPs to serve as a base for such a knowledge graph and integrates with other Materials Science ontologies.
\end{abstract}

\section{Description of the Ontology\daniel{We should add the ontology name here}}

We next describe the proposed ontology. To make this description pedagogical, we describe the proposed ontology with a sequence of paragraph pairs, where the first paragraph introduces the concepts in an intuitive way, and the second paragraph formalizes what is described in the first one.

\paragraph{P1.} Materials Science simulations use a 3-dimensional box, called a \emph{simulation cell}, whose size usually ranges from 1 to 100 \AA{} and contains from Y1 to Y2 atoms. A simulation starts from simulation cell $s_0$ in a time $t_0$, and applies classical dynamics to calculate the atom positions in the next state, $s_1$, defined for the time $t_1 = t_0 + \Delta$. To obtain the energies and forces needed for the classical dynamics, different methods can be used. The most precise one is based on Quantum Mechanics. However, because Quantum Mechanics are is resource intensive, \emph{Machine Learning Interatomic Potentials (MLIPs)} are used as a efficient and accurate approximation. There are several methods to compute MLIPs. One of them is MTP. For example, Jung et al.~\cite{DARUS-3239_2023} published several MTP models.

\paragraph{O1.}
The core concept of the ontology is the $\MLIP$ class, which is the aforementioned \emph{machine-learned model} to calculate energies and forces in a simulation cell. $\MTP$ is a subclass of class $\MLIP$. A $\Simulation$ is associated to multiple simulation cells $\SimulationCell$ with the property $\hasState$. The properties $\initialState$ and $\finalState$ are two sub-properties of $\hasState$ to indicate the initial and final state of a simulation.
