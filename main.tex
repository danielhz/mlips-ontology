\begin{abstract}
Machine learning interatomic potentials (MLIPs) have transformed atomistic
simulations by enabling near \emph{ab initio} accuracy at a fraction of the
computational cost. The field now encompasses a growing ecosystem of algorithms,
training datasets, hyperparameters, and target materials, yet the metadata
needed to systematically compare, reproduce, and build upon MLIP studies
remains scattered across papers, scripts, and ad-hoc file formats. We present
the \MLIPsOntology{}, an OWL\,2~DL ontology that captures the concepts needed
to describe MLIP algorithms, their hyperparameters, training datasets with DFT
provenance, and published benchmarks. The ontology is organized into three
modules---Algorithm, Training Data, and Benchmark---and is designed in alignment
with existing ontologies in materials science (MDO, CMSO/ASMO) and machine
learning (ML-Schema). It includes 27 formal axioms that enforce data
completeness and consistency, including property chains that link trained models
to their algorithms and training data. We demonstrate the ontology through a
running example based on Moment Tensor Potentials and evaluate it through
competency question coverage, OWL reasoning, and comparison with existing
ontologies.

\keywords{Ontology \and Machine learning interatomic potentials \and
  Materials science \and OWL \and FAIR data}
\end{abstract}
