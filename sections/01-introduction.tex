\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
%% ====================================================================

Machine learning interatomic potentials
(MLIPs)~\cite{behler2007hdnnp,bartok2010gap} are models trained to predict the
energy and forces between atoms in a material, enabling simulations at near
quantum-mechanical accuracy but at a fraction of the computational cost of
\emph{ab initio} methods such as density functional theory
(DFT)~\cite{kohn1965dft}. Over the past decade, the field has produced a
growing ecosystem of MLIP algorithms---including
MACE~\cite{batatia2022mace}, ACE~\cite{drautz2019ace},
NequIP~\cite{batzner2022nequip}, M3GNet~\cite{chen2022m3gnet}, and
MTP~\cite{shapeev2016mtp}---each with distinct architectures, hyperparameters,
and application domains.

Researchers routinely face three interrelated challenges: selecting the right
algorithm for a given material and property, constructing suitable training
datasets, and optimizing
hyperparameters~\cite{grabowski2024metalearnerc}. These challenges are
compounded by a \emph{knowledge management problem}: the metadata connecting
MLIP studies---which algorithm was used, with which training set, for which
material system, at what accuracy---is scattered across published papers,
supplementary materials, ad-hoc scripts, and library-specific file formats.

Existing ontologies address parts of this landscape but not the specific needs
of MLIP research. ML ontologies (ML-Schema~\cite{publio2018mlschema},
DMOP~\cite{keet2015dmop}, MEX~\cite{esteves2015mex},
CMO~\cite{foltin2024cmo}) model general ML workflows but not
physics-specific hyperparameters or training set construction strategies.
Materials science ontologies (EMMO~\cite{horsch2020emmo},
MDO~\cite{li2024mdo}, PMDco~\cite{bayerlein2024pmdco},
CMSO/ASMO~\cite{menon2024atomrdf}) model the physical domain but not the
specifics of MLIP training. No existing ontology captures what is needed to
describe the provenance and composition of training datasets, the DFT settings
under which reference calculations were performed, and the algorithm-specific
hyperparameters that govern model accuracy.

In this paper, we present:

\begin{enumerate}
\item The \emph{\MLIPsOntology{}}, an OWL ontology organized into three
  modules (Algorithm, Training Data, Benchmark) that captures the concepts
  specific to MLIP research. The ontology is designed in alignment with MDO,
  CMSO/ASMO, and EMMO, reusing their classes and properties where appropriate
  and extending them where MLIP-specific concepts are needed.

\item The \emph{\MLIPsKG{}}, a structured knowledge graph populated in
  collaboration with domain experts from the META-LEARN
  project~\cite{grabowski2024metalearnerc}, covering major MLIP algorithm
  families, their hyperparameters, training datasets with DFT provenance,
  and published benchmarks. The knowledge graph is publicly queryable via a
  SPARQL endpoint and linked to Wikidata~\cite{vrandecic2014wikidata} for
  broader interoperability.

\item \emph{SHACL shapes} that define data quality constraints for the
  knowledge graph, enforce consistency during data entry, and enable
  form-based editing for domain experts who are not SPARQL specialists.

\item \emph{\MLIPE{}} (Machine Learned Interatomic Potentials Environment), a
  set of domain-specific extension modules for the \Concon{} collaborative
  knowledge graph platform. While \Concon{} provides the general-purpose
  infrastructure (team management, SPARQL endpoints, SHACL-driven editing,
  literature processing), \MLIPE{} adds features specific to MLIP research:
  custom SPARQL extension functions for domain-specific computations (e.g.,
  radial distribution functions, phonon spectra), integration with simulation
  toolkits, and specialized AI extraction agents for the MLIP literature.

\item \emph{\SemASE{}}, a Python library that extends the Atomic Simulation
  Environment (ASE)~\cite{larsen2017ase} with RDF serialization, providing
  a programmatic bridge for feeding simulation data into the knowledge graph.
\end{enumerate}

These resources are developed within the META-LEARN project, an ERC Advanced
Grant effort to create a meta-learning framework for MLIP
generation~\cite{grabowski2024metalearnerc}. META-LEARN aims to systematically
map the space of MLIP algorithms, hyperparameters, training strategies, and
target materials. The ontology and knowledge graph presented here form the
semantic backbone of this effort.
